Small Words, Big Difference

Vocabulary (German – English): Polite phrases for everyday life in Lindenberg

  • Bitte / Bitte schön – Please / You're welcome / Here you go. Note: Never translate 'You're welcome' literally — always reply to Danke with Bitte schön!
  • Danke / Vielen Dank – Thank you / Many thanks
  • Entschuldigung! – Excuse me! / Sorry!
  • Es tut mir leid. – I am sorry.
  • Guten Appetit! – Enjoy your meal!
  • Gesundheit! – Bless you!

Exercise niesen-wort

  • Task: Mina sneezes at the table. What do people say in Germany?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Gesundheit! – Gesundheit! (literally 'health!') is the standard response to a sneeze.
    • ✗ Guten Appetit! – Guten Appetit! is said before a meal, not after a sneeze.
    • ✗ Entschuldigung! – Entschuldigung! is used when excusing yourself or apologizing.
  • Solution: Gesundheit!
  • Explanation: Saying Gesundheit! after a sneeze and Guten Appetit! before eating is part of everyday life in Germany.

Exercise hoeflichkeit-paare

  • Task: Match each situation to the right German phrase.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: You thank someone for a gift. – Vielen Dank!; You arrive five minutes late. – Es tut mir leid.; You start eating dinner together. – Guten Appetit!; Someone says 'Danke!' to you. – Bitte schön!
  • Explanation: Use 'Es tut mir leid' for a genuine apology and 'Entschuldigung' to get attention or pass by.

Exercise hoeflichkeit-wahl

  • Task: Choose the right German phrase for each scene in Lindenberg.
  • Answer format: choose a word for each gap
  • How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters wrong, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation wrong
  • Items (start → solution):
  1. You want to get past someone on the bus. → Entschuldigung, darf ich vorbei?
  2. Frau Weber hands you a glass of water and you reply. → Vielen Dank, Frau Weber!
  3. Lukas thanks you for helping with his bike. → Bitte schön, gern geschehen!
  • Explanation: Bitte schön replies to Danke, and Entschuldigung politely gets someone's attention.
