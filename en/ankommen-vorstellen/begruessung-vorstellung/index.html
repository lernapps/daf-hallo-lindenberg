Arriving at Lindenberg Station

Peter (12) is moving to Lindenberg with his parents for a year! At 3:00 p.m. (15:00) they step off the train — waiting on the platform are family friends Frau Weber (Ms. Weber), Lukas (12), and Mina (9).

Exercise

It is 3:00 p.m. in the afternoon. Use the English clues (Morgen ≈ morning, Nacht ≈ night): which polite greeting fits Frau Weber?

  • Guten Tag, Frau Weber! right answer

    Guten Tag ('Good day') is the standard polite greeting from midday to early evening.

  • Guten Morgen, Frau Weber!

    Guten Morgen is only used in the morning (until about 11:00), not at 3:00 p.m.

  • Gute Nacht, Frau Weber!

    Gute Nacht is only said before going to sleep, not when meeting someone at the station.

Polite greetings in Germany follow the time of day: Guten Morgen (morning), Guten Tag (daytime), and Guten Abend (evening).

Greetings and Introducing Yourself

The first card in Peter's Notebook matches each polite greeting to its time of day (after 10 p.m. people still say Guten Abend; Gute Nacht is only for bedtime). With peers, Hallo! works any time.

Good to know: ß, capital letters, and Gute Nacht

The letter ß in heiße sounds like a sharp 'ss' — click Special characters below an input to type it. All German nouns are capitalized (Jahre = years, alt = old). And Gute Nacht has no '-n' because Nacht is feminine (you will see why later).

Practice Your First Conversations

Exercise

Match each German phrase to its English meaning.

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: Guten Abend! – Good evening!; Auf Wiedersehen! – Goodbye! (formal); Ich komme aus Deutschland. – I come from Germany.; Tschüss! – Bye! (informal)

Use Auf Wiedersehen with adults in formal situations and Tschüss with friends and peers.

Solution

Exercise

Complete Peter's introduction on the platform.

Hint

Use the verbs from the phrase list above and the 'Special characters' button for ß.

  1. Hi! My name is Peter. → …! Ich … Peter.
  2. I come from England. → Ich … aus England.
  3. I am 12 years old. → Ich … 12 Jahre ….
Solution
  1. Hallo! Ich heiße Peter.
  2. Ich komme aus England.
  3. Ich bin 12 Jahre alt.

In German, when giving your age with 'Jahre', always include 'alt' at the end: Ich bin 12 Jahre alt.

Exercise

Now Peter's future classmate Nadia (12) introduces herself. Write both sentences in German.

Hint

Start both sentences with 'Ich …' and use 'Deutschland' for Germany.

  1. My name is Nadia.
  2. I come from Germany.
Solution
  1. Ich heiße Nadia. (also right: Ich bin Nadia.)
  2. Ich komme aus Deutschland.
