Numbers and Euro Prices

Picture: Diagram of German two-digit numbers: 24 spoken as vier-und-zwanzig (ones + und + tens), plus price tag 1,24 € using a German decimal comma. Caption: Peter's Notebook: ones before tens and the comma in German Euro prices.

Vocabulary (German – English): Key numbers and shopping phrases

  • null, eins, zwei, drei, vier, fünf – 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
  • sechs, sieben, acht, neun, zehn – 6, 7, 8, 9, 10
  • elf, zwölf, dreizehn, sechzehn, siebzehn, zwanzig – 11, 12, 13, 16, 17, 20. Note: 16 (sechzehn) and 17 (siebzehn) shorten the stem; eins drops -s in 21/31 (einunddreißig).
  • dreißig, vierzig, fünfzig, sechzig, siebzig, achtzig, neunzig, hundert – 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100. Note: Only dreißig (30) uses ß; all other tens end in -zig.
  • die Telefonnummer – the phone number. Example: Meine Telefonnummer ist null-eins-sieben-zwei, dreißig, zwölf. – Phone numbers are read digit by digit or in two-digit pairs: 0172 30 12.
  • Was kostet das? – How much does that cost?. Example: Das kostet drei Euro zwanzig. – That costs three euros twenty.

Exercise zahl-drehen

  • Task: A bus ticket costs 2,45 € (two euros forty-five). How do you say 45 in German?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ fünfundvierzig – Ones first (fünf), then 'und', then tens (vierzig).
    • ✗ vierundfünfzig – vierundfünfzig is 54 — a classic digit swap!
    • ✗ vierzigfünf – German puts ones before tens linked by 'und'.
  • Solution: fünfundvierzig
  • Explanation: From 21 up, German always names the ones digit before the tens digit.
  • Link: ../?via=tutor#zahl-drehen

Exercise preis-rechnen

  • Task: Peter buys a ticket for 'drei Euro zwanzig' and a roll for 'achtzig Cent'. How many euros does Peter pay in total?
  • Answer format: type a number (€)
  • Solution: 4 €
  • Explanation: 3,20 € + 0,80 € = 4,00 €. Germany uses a comma to separate euros and cents.
  • Link: ../?via=tutor#preis-rechnen

Exercise zahlen-zuordnen

  • Task: Match each German number or spoken phone number to the right digits.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: siebzehn – 17; einunddreißig – 31; siebenundsechzig – 67; null-eins-sieben-zwei (mobile prefix) – 0172
  • Explanation: Read ones first, then tens: siebenundsechzig = 7 + 60 = 67.
  • Link: ../?via=tutor#zahlen-zuordnen

Exercise zahlen-schreiben

  • Task: Write both numbers as a single German word.
  • Answer format: write the whole answer in German
  • How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters right with a hint, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation ignored
  • Items (start → solution):
  1. 25 → fünfundzwanzig
    • zwanzigundfünf: German puts the ones digit first: fünf + und + zwanzig.
  2. 30 → dreißig
    • dreizig: 30 is the one exception spelled with ß (use the Special characters button).
    • dreissig: In Germany, 30 is spelled with ß: dreißig.
  • The app's hint: For 25 use ones + und + tens, and remember the ß in 30.
  • Explanation: Great! Now that you know numbers and prices, next Peter adds times and days to his Peter's Notebook calendar.
  • How to help: the app checks word by word against the solutions above and names mistakes of form (e.g. accents) with the child's words. Do not give the solution: ask about the rule (ending, gender, tense) or about a similar sentence of the page. If the app takes a version of the child's as wrong that you think is right, tell the child: the app knows only the versions above.
