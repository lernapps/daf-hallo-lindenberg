Structure of a Short Message

After this page you can write a short invitation or reply to friends in German. Every short message has three parts: Greeting (Hallo Nadia,), Body (when, where, question), and Sign-off (Liebe Grüße, Peter / Bis Samstag, Peter). Start by ordering Peter's reply to Lukas!

Exercise

Put the four parts of Peter's reply to Lukas in the right order.

Give each entry its place: 1 for the first.

Right answer: 1. Hallo Lukas,, 2. danke für deine Nachricht!, 3. Ich komme um 16:00 Uhr zum Stadtpark., 4. Bis später, Peter

Start with the greeting, reply clearly to the invitation, and finish with a sign-off and your name.

Writing an Invitation

Peter's invitation to Nadia

'Hallo Nadia, hast du am Samstag um 15:00 Uhr Zeit? Ich möchte ins Eiscafé am Marktplatz gehen. Kommst du mit? Liebe Grüße, Peter'

Complete and Write Short Messages

Exercise

Where does the verb belong? Pick the natural German sentence!

  • Am Samstag gehe ich ins Kino. right answer

    'Am Samstag' is position 1, so the verb 'gehe' stays right in position 2.

  • Am Samstag ich gehe ins Kino.

    Here 'gehe' slips to position 3 — that is English word order, not German.

  • Gehe am Samstag ich ins Kino.

    In a statement (ending with a period), the verb belongs in position 2, not position 1.

When a time phrase comes first, subject and verb swap places so the verb stays in position 2.

Exercise

Complete Peter's invitation to Nadia.

Hint

Capitalize nouns, use Special characters for ü/ß in Grüße, and keep the verb in position 2.

  1. Do you have time on Saturday? → Hast du am Samstag …?
  2. At 3:00 p.m. I am going to the ice-cream parlor. → Um 15:00 Uhr … ich ins Eiscafé.
  3. Love / Cheers, Peter → Liebe …, Peter
Solution
  1. Hast du am Samstag Zeit?
  2. Um 15:00 Uhr gehe ich ins Eiscafé.
  3. Liebe Grüße, Peter

Zeit and Grüße are capitalized nouns, and the verb 'gehe' comes immediately after 'Um 15:00 Uhr'.
