Greetings and Introducing Yourself

The first card in Peter's Notebook matches each polite greeting to its time of day (after 10 p.m. people still say Guten Abend; Gute Nacht is only for bedtime). With peers, Hallo! works any time.

Picture: Four time cards for German greetings: 06–11 Morning Guten Morgen, 11–18 Daytime Guten Tag, 18–22 Evening Guten Abend, Bedtime Gute Nacht; informal Hallo and Tschüss; formal bye Auf Wiedersehen. Caption: Peter's Notebook: matching each greeting to the time of day.

Remember: Good to know: ß, capital letters, and Gute Nacht

The letter ß in heiße sounds like a sharp 'ss' — click Special characters below an input to type it. All German nouns are capitalized (Jahre = years, alt = old). And Gute Nacht has no '-n' because Nacht is feminine (you will see why later).

Vocabulary (German – English): Peter's first phrases at the station

  • Hallo! / Guten Tag! – Hi! / Good day! (polite). Example: Hallo Mina! Guten Tag, Frau Weber! – Hi Mina! Hello, Frau Weber!
  • Tschüss! / Auf Wiedersehen! – Bye! (informal) / Goodbye! (formal)
  • Ich heiße … – My name is …. Example: Ich heiße Lukas. – My name is Lukas.
  • Ich komme aus … – I come from …. Example: Ich komme aus Deutschland. – I come from Germany.
  • Ich bin … Jahre alt. – I am … years old.. Example: Ich bin 9 Jahre alt. – I am 9 years old.
  • Deutschland – Germany

Exercise begruessung-nachmittag

  • Task: It is 3:00 p.m. in the afternoon. Use the English clues (Morgen ≈ morning, Nacht ≈ night): which polite greeting fits Frau Weber?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Guten Tag, Frau Weber! – Guten Tag ('Good day') is the standard polite greeting from midday to early evening.
    • ✗ Guten Morgen, Frau Weber! – Guten Morgen is only used in the morning (until about 11:00), not at 3:00 p.m.
    • ✗ Gute Nacht, Frau Weber! – Gute Nacht is only said before going to sleep, not when meeting someone at the station.
  • Solution: Guten Tag, Frau Weber!
  • Explanation: Polite greetings in Germany follow the time of day: Guten Morgen (morning), Guten Tag (daytime), and Guten Abend (evening).
