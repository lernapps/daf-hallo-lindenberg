Context: the page “Informal du vs. Formal Sie”

Choose appropriately between informal du and formal Sie in Germany.

Two Words for 'You'

After this page you can choose confidently between informal du and formal Sie (always capitalized). The next morning Peter meets his class teacher Herr Braun (Mr. Braun) outside the classroom.

  • Exercise du-sie-lehrer (below)

When to Use du vs. Sie

The second card in Peter's Notebook splits people into two clear zones: green for the informal du circle (peers, friends, family) and blue for the formal Sie circle (teachers, unfamiliar adults).

Picture: Two zones: green left box du (friends, kids, teens, family with first name) and blue right box Sie (teachers, unfamiliar adults, staff with Frau or Herr plus last name). Caption: Peter's Notebook: when in doubt, start with formal Sie.

Remember: Why is formal Sie always capitalized?

Lowercase sie means 'she' or 'they' — only capital Sie means polite 'you'. Family friends often offer du to kids themselves ('Sag gern du zu mir').

Exercise du-sie-lehrer

  • Task: How does Peter politely ask his teacher Herr Braun how he is?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Wie geht es Ihnen, Herr Braun? – In Germany, students address teachers with formal Sie/Ihnen and Herr/Frau + last name.
    • ✗ Wie geht es dir, Braun? – Using du and just a last name sounds impolite to a teacher.
    • ✗ Hallo, wie heißt du? – That asks for a name with informal du instead of asking how he is with formal Sie.
  • Solution: Wie geht es Ihnen, Herr Braun?
  • Explanation: In Germany, address adults outside your family with formal Sie and Frau/Herr + last name.
